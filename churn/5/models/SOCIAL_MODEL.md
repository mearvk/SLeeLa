# /5 · Social model — common counts and national lengths

The **social model** here reads *a Line as a plain measure of a society*: a
**common count** (how many) or a **national length** (how far). These are
**OBSERVED** — published tallies and surveyed extents for a stated period, from
U.S. primary sources. This is the pillar the [`BASIS.md`](../BASIS.md) rests on:
a medium is firm only if it can count itself and measure its own reach.

## The three pillars, read through Lines

- **Workers (count).** The structural counts (50 states, 3,143 counties) and the
  head count (331,449,281) are how the substrate *counts itself* — the pillar in
  scope in [`BASIS.md`](../BASIS.md).
- **Work (length).** The national lengths — borders, rivers, the Interstate
  system — are the *measured reach* the substrate actually spans and uses.
- **Excellence (a principled count).** **Two senators per state** (a count of
  100) is the model's example of a Line that *earns its place*: it encodes
  **equal voice**, a principle, not merely a quantity. Its Spark says so.

## The distinction the model keeps

A social Line is **OBSERVED**: a published count or a surveyed length for a
stated period. It is not EXACT (it can change as the society changes) and it is
not a claim about why the number is what it is. A count can encode a principle
(equal voice), but the *principle* lives in the Spark (a guide), while the
*number* lives in the value (evidence). `VALUE ≠ SPARK`; `ASSOCIATION ≠
CAUSATION`.
