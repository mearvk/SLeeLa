# Not Observed Is Not No

A careful helper has a rule: **say only what you saw, and say where you looked.**

The helper has a **field**: the one place it looked. A list says:

| thing | place |
|-------|-------|
| ball | red box |
| book | blue box |

Someone asks, "Is there a kite?" There are three honest ideas:

- "Yes, I saw it." (not true here)
- "No kite exists anywhere." (a guess, too big!)
- **"Not observed in this field."** (true)

The third answer is the careful one. A missing entry only tells you about the
place you looked.

## Evidence

A good claim comes with proof: **which entry** you looked at. "The ball is in the
red box, because I opened the red box." A claim with no entry is refused.

## Steps follow each other

Step 2 may use step 1. Step 1 may not use step 2. This is called **sequitur**:
each step follows the last.

## Questions

1. A list shows ball and book. Is "there is no pencil" a good claim? What is better?
2. Why does a helper refuse "step 3" when the last accepted step was 1?

**Answers:** 1. Not good. Better: "a pencil is not observed in this field."
2. Step 3 skips step 2, so it does not follow the last accepted step.
