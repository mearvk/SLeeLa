# /2 · Social model — Structures of a working society

The **social model** reads recent US labor and social figures as *Structures*:
standing quantities that describe a society at a moment. Unlike a physical
constant, a social structure is **OBSERVED** — a real published figure for a
stated period — and unlike a math constant it is neither exact nor timeless.

## What the model is *for*

These structures describe **a working society** — who is in the labor force, how
much output each working hour lays down, and what a median household earns.
That makes the social model the empirical counterpart to the BASIS document
([`../BASIS.md`](../BASIS.md)), which sets out the **Medium requirement for
Workers, Work and Excellence — Workers**: the standing quantities here are the
real-world *workers-and-work* structures the requirement reasons about.

- **Participation** (62.5%) and **employment–population ratio** (59.9%) are
  *workers* structures — how much of the society is at work.
- **Productivity** (+2.7%) is a *work-and-excellence* structure — how much value
  a working hour lays down, the measurable face of excellence in the aggregate.
- **Median income** ($83,730) is the *reward-of-work* structure.

## The discipline the model keeps

Every social structure is tagged **OBSERVED** and carries its own **period** and
**source**. These are published statistics, not definitions and not causal
claims: `ASSOCIATION ≠ CAUSATION`. Drawing a relation between two of them (e.g.
productivity and income) is a **MODEL**, stated to be argued with — never a fact
asserted about any person.

## Provenance

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (bls.gov), U.S. Census Bureau (census.gov), and
the Federal Reserve / FRED, retrieved 2026-09. Each structure in the data file
names its exact release.
