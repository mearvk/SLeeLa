# 03 — China: concepts and unification

*China's calendar concepts, and how China unified onto the Gregorian civil
calendar while retaining its traditional calendar as a cultural layer.*

## The Chinese concepts

The traditional Chinese calendar is a **lunisolar** system — the same class of
problem the Greeks addressed (document 00), solved with a distinctively Chinese
apparatus:

- **Lunar months.** Each month begins on the day of the astronomical new moon,
  so months are 29 or 30 days long and the month boundary is defined by
  observation/computation of the Moon, not by a fixed rule.
- **Solar terms (二十四节气, the 24 jiéqì).** The solar year is divided into 24
  equal segments of the Sun's ecliptic longitude. These terms (e.g. the
  solstices and equinoxes) tie the calendar to the Sun and govern agriculture.
- **Leap months (闰月).** When needed to keep the lunar months aligned with the
  solar terms, an intercalary month is inserted — the Chinese analogue of the
  Greek intercalary month and the Metonic correction.
- **The sexagenary cycle (干支).** A 60-unit cycle formed by pairing 10 Heavenly
  Stems with 12 Earthly Branches, used to name years, months, days. The familiar
  **12-year zodiac** (rat, ox, tiger, …) is the Earthly-Branch layer.
- **Era/regnal reckoning.** Years were counted within an emperor's reign era
  (年号), so chronology was anchored to the sovereign.

## Unification onto the Gregorian civil calendar

China did not *reform* the Julian/Gregorian line; it **joined** it, in two
steps:

1. **1912 — Republic of China.** On founding the Republic, China adopted the
   **Gregorian calendar** for official and civil purposes, counting years from
   the Republic's establishment (Minguo era) alongside Gregorian months and days.
   The traditional lunisolar calendar continued for festivals.
2. **1949 — People's Republic of China.** The PRC confirmed and **unified
   nationwide** use of the Gregorian calendar (公历, the "common calendar") for
   all civil, administrative, and international purposes, with CE year numbering.
   The traditional calendar (农历, the "agricultural calendar") was kept as the
   cultural/festival layer — Chinese New Year, the Mid-Autumn Festival, and other
   observances are still set by it.

## The two-layer settlement

The Chinese resolution is a clean **two-layer** model, and it is exactly the
pattern the Unified Calendar (document 99) generalizes:

- **Civil layer:** Gregorian — for law, commerce, science, and interchange.
- **Cultural layer:** the traditional lunisolar calendar — for festivals and
  heritage, computed from new moons and solar terms.

The same instant carries both labels; neither replaces the other. China thus
contributes the key integration concept: **a shared civil calendar does not
require abolishing local/cultural calendars — it overlays them.**

## What carries forward to the Unified Calendar

- **Lunisolar observation** (new-moon month starts, 24 solar terms) — a precise
  astronomical calendar kept as a cultural layer.
- **The two-layer settlement** — Gregorian civil calendar + retained traditional
  calendar — the model document 99 adopts for reconciling the world's calendars.
