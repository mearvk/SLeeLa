## The work and its story

The allusion is to **_The Nine Gates of the Kingdom of Shadows_**, a fictional 17th-century-style
occult book that is central to:

- Arturo Pérez-Reverte's 1993 novel **_El Club Dumas_** (_The Club Dumas_), and
- Roman Polanski's 1999 film **_The Ninth Gate_** (starring Johnny Depp).

In the story, rare-book dealer **Dean Corso** is hired by collector **Boris Balkan** to
authenticate the grimoire. Three copies survive; Balkan believes only one is genuine, because his
attempts to use it have failed. Corso travels across Europe comparing the nine woodcut engravings
across the three copies — amid a string of deaths and strange events, and with help from a
mysterious young woman (the "Girl"). The engravings are the key to the mystery: in the book they
were drawn by the artist **Francisco Solé**, and they convincingly imitate period woodcuts while
using genuine occult imagery.

## The signed-plate device (AT vs. LCF)

The grimoire's puzzle turns on a forgery mechanic. Of the nine plates in each copy, some are
marked with the initials of the book's nominal engraver and some with the initials **"LCF"** — a
hidden attribution to a different, darker hand (read as _Lucifer_). The "LCF" plates differ from
their ordinary counterparts in small, deliberate details (a castle with three towers rather than
four; a key held in the left hand rather than the right; an extra arrow; a board of the opposite
colour). Finding the **authentic** plates — scattered across the three copies — and reading them
in the correct order is the real task. A substituted **forgery** of the ninth plate is the central
illusion.

## The nine engravings — what they depict and their occult roots

Described in prose only; the plates themselves are not reproduced. Numbered as in the grimoire.
Each engraving contains a door (the nine gates of an initiation) and draws on a recognizable tarot
or alchemical image.

| # | Depicted scene | Occult / tarot resonance |
|---|----------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | A knight rides toward a castle, finger to his lips | "To know and keep silent" — the occult maxim of discretion; castle has 3 towers (LCF) vs 4 (AT): spiritual vs material destination |
| 2 | An old man with a dog holds two keys | **The Hermit** — withdrawal, contemplation, the keys to insight; left-hand (LCF) vs right-hand (AT) keys evoke the "Left-Hand Path"; bears the Hebrew sign for nine |
| 3 | A traveller walks toward a bridge under a Cupid-like archer | **The Fool** setting out; the arrow as alchemical "universal solvent"; one arrow (AT) vs two (LCF) → "as above, so below" |
| 4 | A jester at the entrance to a labyrinth, dice before him | The labyrinth as the underworld / subconscious and its trial; exit open (LCF) vs bricked up (AT); the dice faces read as 6-6-6 |
| 5 | A man counts coins while Death stands behind with hourglass and pitchfork | **Death** as transformation; the hourglass run out (LCF) vs full (AT); "you can't take it with you" |
| 6 | A man hangs upside-down by one ankle; a flaming sword from a tower | **The Hanged Man** — willing ordeal, metamorphosis; hangs by the left foot (LCF) vs right (AT) |
| 7 | A king and a peasant play chess; two dogs fight; a moon in the window | **The Moon** — subconscious, illusion; black/white dogs and board as reconciled duality; "the disciple surpasses the master" |
| 8 | A kneeling figure struck by a knight's mace, Wheel of Fortune behind | **The Wheel of Fortune**; an alchemical "beheading" (dissolution); the attacker haloed (LCF) marking a necessary step |
| 9 | A woman rides a seven-headed dragon, reading the book, a castle beyond | **The Tower** (castle in flames — the forgery) vs the true **The Sun** (light behind the tower): destruction vs enlightenment; "from Darkness comes Light" |

The plate captions are brief epigrams (e.g. the ninth reads, in the common English rendering,
"Now I know that from Darkness comes Light"). The throughline the analysis draws out is an
**initiatory arc** — silence, the keys, setting out, the labyrinth, death, the ordeal, reconciled
opposites, dissolution, and finally light — i.e. spiritual growth rather than the raw power the
antagonist seeks.
