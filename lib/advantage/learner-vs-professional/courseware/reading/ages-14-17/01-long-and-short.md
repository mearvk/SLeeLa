# Long and Short in Plain Words

**Long** means you own something. You make money if its price goes up.
**Short** means you sold something you borrowed and must buy back later. You make
money if its price goes down.

Profit per share:

- long: sell price - buy price
- short: sell price - buy-back price (called the **cover** price)

## The mirror law

Use the same prices. If a long position makes +100 000, the short position
makes -100 000. Same numbers, opposite sign. For 100 shares at 5000 cents each,
a price move to 4000 gives:

- long: (4000 - 5000) x 100 = **-100 000** cents
- short: (5000 - 4000) x 100 = **+100 000** cents

## The risks are not mirror images

A long position can lose at most what it paid. The price cannot go below 1 cent,
so 100 shares bought at 5000 lose at most (5000 - 1) x 100 = **499 900** cents.

A short position has **no ceiling**. Short 100 shares at 5000 (proceeds 500 000).
If the price rises to 15 000, covering costs 1 500 000, a loss of **1 000 000**
cents. That is **twice** the proceeds, and a higher price loses more.

This is why the shorting side of a book needs extra care: loss limits,
collateral, and a margin check (see the adult readers).

## Questions

1. Short 10 shares at 200 cents. Cover at 150. Profit?
2. Short 10 shares at 200. Cover at 350. Profit?
3. Why can a long loss be capped but a short loss cannot?

**Answers:** 1. (200 - 150) x 10 = **+500**. 2. (200 - 350) x 10 = **-1500**.
3. A price has a floor (it cannot go below zero) but no ceiling.
