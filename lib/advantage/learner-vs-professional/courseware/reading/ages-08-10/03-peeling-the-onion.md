# Peeling the Onion

An onion has layers. You peel one, then the next. Solving a problem can work the
same way: **one layer at a time, each layer follows the one before.**

## The toy shop

Shop A sells a kite for **12 coins**.

| layer | what we do | result |
|-------|------------|--------|
| start | the price | 12 |
| add | bag fee of 2 | 14 |
| take away | coupon of 3 | 11 |

Shop A costs **11 coins**.

Shop B sells the same kite for **11 coins** with a **2 coin** bag fee and no
coupon: 11 + 2 = **13 coins**.

Shop A is cheaper by **2 coins**.

## A helper checks your work

Your friend gets 14 for Shop A. They forgot the coupon. Do not just change your
number to match. Look at the coupon again, then fix it.

If two shops cost the **same**, our rule is: **the first one listed wins.** Write
the rule down before you start, so nobody argues.

**Try it:** Pick two toys with prices, a fee and a coupon. Peel the layers.
