# Unit 2 (Children): AND, OR, NOT, Together

**You will learn:** to combine facts with AND, OR and NOT, and to check your
answer against a friend. **Use with:** [lesson 4, agreement](../series/README.md).

## The play table

Our field is the toy shelf from Unit 1, now with one more thing: a **green
block** on the shelf, not in a box.

| thing | where it is |
|-------|-------------|
| ball | red box |
| book | blue box |
| block | shelf |
| green block | shelf |

## Part 1: Two facts at once

Say true or false. Remember: AND needs both, OR needs one, NOT flips.

1. The ball is in the red box AND the block is on the shelf.
2. The ball is in the blue box OR the book is in the blue box.
3. NOT (the book is in the red box).
4. NOT (the ball is in the red box).
5. (The block is on the shelf AND the green block is on the shelf) AND the ball
   is in the red box.
6. The ball is in the blue box OR the book is in the red box.

## Part 2: Build your own

7. Write one AND statement that is true and one that is false.
8. Write one OR statement that is true even though one part is false.

## Part 3: Check with a friend (agreement)

Two friends each write down where every thing is, without peeking at each
other. Then they compare, thing by thing.

- If both lists match for a thing, put a tick.
- If they differ, **both look at the shelf again** for that thing only.
- When every thing has a tick, you agree.

9. Friend A wrote: ball in the blue box. Friend B wrote: ball in the red box.
   What do they do next?
10. Friend A listed four things. Friend B listed three. How do they find out
    which thing B did not write?

The Device Instructor does this in lesson 4. Run it with a grown-up and find
the line that says "-> re-observe".

## Answer key

1. True. 2. True (the book is in the blue box). 3. True. 4. False.
5. True (all three parts are true). 6. False (both parts are false).
7. Any AND whose parts are both true is true; any AND with one false part is false.
8. For example: "the ball is in the blue box OR the block is on the shelf".
9. Both look at the ball again. They do not just change the answer to match.
10. Compare the lists; the thing with no match on B's list is the one B missed.
    The Instructor says "master only" or "student only" for this.
