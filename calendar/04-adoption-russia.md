# 04 — Russia: concepts and the 1918 switch

*Russia's calendar concepts, and its late — and therefore large — switch from
Julian to Gregorian in 1918.*

## The Russian concepts

Russia's calendar history is the story of a long, deliberate attachment to the
**Julian baseline** (document 01) through the Orthodox Church:

- **The Julian civil calendar.** Russia kept the Julian calendar for civil life
  centuries after Catholic Europe had moved to Gregorian, because the Russian
  Orthodox Church computed its liturgical year — above all the date of **Pascha
  (Easter)** — on the Julian reckoning and the Greek Metonic computus (document
  00).
- **The Byzantine Anno Mundi era.** Before Peter the Great, Russia numbered years
  **from the supposed creation of the world** (Anno Mundi, epoch 5509 BCE) and
  began the year on **1 September**.
- **Peter the Great's reform (1700).** Peter aligned Russia's *year count and
  year start* with Europe — adopting **Anno Domini** numbering and a **1 January**
  new year — but pointedly **kept the Julian leap rule**. So Russia used European
  year numbers on Julian arithmetic, widening the O.S./N.S. gap over time.
- **The liturgical calendar persists.** The Russian Orthodox Church still uses
  the Julian calendar for its fixed feasts today — which is why Orthodox
  Christmas falls on 7 January (Gregorian) = 25 December (Julian).

## The 1918 switch (the large, late exception)

By the 20th century Russia was the last major European state still on Julian, and
the accumulated drift had grown to **13 days**. Shortly after the October
Revolution, the Soviet government decreed the change:

- **Wednesday 31 January 1918 (O.S.) was followed by Thursday 14 February 1918
  (N.S.)** — **13 days deleted**, the largest skip among the major adoptions
  precisely because Russia waited the longest (document 02 explains why the skip
  grows with time).
- This is the reason the **"October Revolution"** (25 October O.S.) is
  commemorated in **November** (7 November N.S.) on the modern calendar.

## Why it belongs in the ordered exceptions

Against the Julian baseline, Russia is **item 7** in the ordered list (README):
not a new rule, but the same Gregorian correction adopted at the latest major
date and therefore with the largest day-skip. Russia makes the collection's
central arithmetic vivid — **the longer a polity held the Julian baseline, the
more days it had to delete to join the Gregorian line.**

## What carries forward to the Unified Calendar

- **The civil/liturgical split.** Like China's civil/cultural split (document
  03), Russia ended with a **Gregorian civil calendar** and a **retained Julian
  liturgical calendar** — another instance of the two-layer settlement document
  99 generalizes.
- **The O.S./N.S. offset as a function of date.** Russia's 13-day skip is a
  direct, checkable consequence of the Julian-vs-Gregorian drift at 1918 — the
  kind of conversion the model in `src/` performs.
