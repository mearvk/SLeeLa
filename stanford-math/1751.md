# 1751 — Modifier Document

A dated modifier on the stanford-math angle. It fixes one principle: **unified,
not single.**

## Anchor

**1751.** Volume 1 of the *Encyclopédie, ou Dictionnaire raisonné des sciences,
des arts et des métiers* (Diderot and d'Alembert) appeared in June 1751, led by
d'Alembert's *Discours préliminaire*. Many named contributors; one coherent
work. The archive takes it as a citable precedent, not a borrowed authority.

- Diderot & d'Alembert, *Encyclopédie*, Tome 1 (Paris, June 1751).
- d'Alembert, *Discours préliminaire de l'Encyclopédie* (1751).

## Principle: unified, not single

The archive is one body built of many faithful records. A record stands on its
own, but no single record carries the standing of the whole. The group's
wellness is its coherence: every entry sourced, every author credited, every
boundary marked. Unity is earned by honest parts, not asserted over them.

- **Unified.** Many records compose into one archive (`SMArchive`). The whole is
  greater than any part.
- **Not single.** No lone record claims the authority of the group. None is
  elevated above the rest; none is dropped for convenience.

This modifier ranks nothing and no one. It divides no group of people. It
divides only roles: part from whole, record from archive.
