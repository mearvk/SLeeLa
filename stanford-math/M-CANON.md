## 1. The premise, stated plainly

The M-premise is a single idea in ordinary language:

> **Conduct can be *favorable* the way a reaction can be favorable.**

That is the whole of it. It takes one honest intuition from thermodynamics —
that some changes are *favored* and proceed on their own, while others are not —
and asks whether a parallel can be *felt* about human conduct: that some ways of
acting "land" more naturally, carry further, and are more welcome than others.

Everything else below is an unpacking of that one sentence. **M** is simply the
name the premise gives to *"net favorability of conduct."* It is a figure of
speech — a lens — not a measured quantity.

## 2. The terms, translated honestly

The premise arrives wrapped in coined vocabulary. Stated plainly, each term is a
metaphor pointing at an ordinary meaning. Nothing here is a defined physical
variable.

| Coined term | Plain meaning (as a metaphor) | Real thing it gestures toward |
|-------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Thermodynamic Favor** | The sense that some conduct is "favored" and flows on its own, like a spontaneous reaction. | Spontaneity: a process is favored when its free-energy change is negative. |
| **Favor toward the Property of Assignment** | Preferring conduct that *assigns* things rightly — gives credit, value, and responsibility where they belong. | Attribution / fair allocation (the archive's own integrity rule). |
| **How Travel lands on Subject** | How an action, once set in motion, *arrives* — where it comes to rest and on whom. | Reaching a resting state (equilibrium); an action's actual effect on people. |
| **Favor as on Newton** | Treating "favor" with the seriousness Newton gave to force — as a directed tendency, not a mood. | A directed tendency toward a lower-energy / more-settled state. |
| **M = Principalism toward net Conduct Becoming** | M is the *principle* of conduct becoming better on balance — net good over net harm. | A direction-of-change idea, like the sign of ΔG, used only by analogy. |
| **The Dance between Merriment and the Sonus** | The pleasant back-and-forth between joy and sound/harmony — conduct that is *enjoyed*. | Resonance / harmony, used figuratively, not as a frequency. |

> The honest translation of the whole phrase: *"Good conduct feels favored and
> settles well, the way a spontaneous process does — and that feeling is worth
> naming."* That is a value statement. It is reasonable to hold. It is **not** a
> law of physics, and this document does not pretend it is.

## 3. The M-premise, in one compound statement

Putting the translations together, plainly:

> **M-premise.** *Let M name the net favorability of a course of conduct. The
> premise holds that conduct tends to "land" well — to be carried forward and
> welcomed — in proportion to how fairly it assigns credit and value (the
> Property of Assignment) and how much it increases shared enjoyment (the Dance
> between Merriment and the Sonus). In this analogy, more-favorable conduct plays
> the role that a negative free-energy change plays for a spontaneous reaction:
> it is the direction things settle toward on their own.*

This is the premise "stated as plain and thorough" as requested. It is a stated
analogy, offered as a way of thinking — explicitly a premise, not a proof.

## 4. Why there is no "M-equation" (the honest boundary)

The request asked for a *compound Newtonian M-equation* and a *covalent-bond
series over money, capitalism, and credit.* This document deliberately does not
provide those as formulas, and it says why — plainly:

- **M is not a measured quantity.** "Net favorability of conduct" has no agreed
  unit, instrument, or dataset. Writing `M = ...` would invent a law with no
  origin, which the archive's one hard rule forbids.
- **There is no covalent bond between chemistry and credit.** A covalent bond is
  a real, specific thing — a shared pair of electrons between atoms. "American
  money, capitalism, and credit" is a real but entirely different domain.
  Notating one as the other would be a false attribution dressed as rigor. The
  archive records a reach *only where the record supports it*, and marks where
  the reach stops. **It stops here.**

Saying this out loud *is* the integrity goal in action: record the idea fully,
credit it honestly as a premise, and refuse to inflate it into a law.

## 5. Where the real science actually lives (anchors you can cite)

The M-premise *gestures* toward real, citable science. Here is the genuine
article — each already recorded faithfully in the canons — so the idea is
anchored to things that are true:

**Favorability / spontaneity (the honest core of "Thermodynamic Favor"):**

- **Gibbs free energy** — `G = H - T S`; a process at constant T, P is favored
  when `ΔG < 0`. *(SMThermoEquations; J. Willard Gibbs, 1873.)*
- **De Donder chemical affinity** — `A = -sum_i nu_i mu_i`, with `A dxi >= 0`;
  the thermodynamic *driving force* of a reaction. *(SMThermoEquations;
  Théophile De Donder, 1922.)*
- **Chemical / phase equilibrium** — `A = 0` (`sum_i nu_i mu_i = 0`); where the
  "travel lands," i.e. the resting state. *(SMThermoEquations; Gibbs; De Donder.)*

**Real covalent bonding (the honest core of "Covalent Bond series"):**

- Covalent bonding is the sharing of electron pairs between atoms (Lewis, 1916;
  Heitler–London, 1927; Pauling, *The Nature of the Chemical Bond*, 1939). These
  belong to a chemistry canon, not to money — and are offered here only to show
  where the real term lives.

**Real money, capitalism, and credit (the honest core of the finance strand):**

- **Quantity theory of money** — `M V = P Q`. *(Irving Fisher, 1911.)*
- **Compound interest / continuous compounding** — `A = P e^(r t)`.
- **Present value** — `PV = sum CF_t / (1 + r)^t`.
- **Fisher equation** — `i ≈ r + pi` (nominal ≈ real + inflation). *(Irving Fisher.)*
- **Money multiplier** — `m = 1 / r` (reserve ratio `r`).

> If you want, these finance and chemistry equations can each be added to the
> canons as **real, attributed entries** (a finance/economics canon and a
> covalent-bonding chemistry canon). That is the faithful way to honor the
> money/capitalism/credit and covalent-bond strands of the premise — as genuine
> science, properly credited, rather than as a metaphor in equation's clothing.

## 7. The Pace premise — "things arrive at the Speed of the National Economy"

A companion speculation, recorded in the same spirit as the M-premise: stated in
full, translated honestly, and anchored to the real economics it gestures
toward. **It, too, is a premise — not a recorded law — and is not entered into
any equation canon.**

### 7.1 The premise, stated plainly

> **Pace premise.** *Things arrive at the speed of the national economy.* The
> speculation is that **throughput and time co-exist at a pace set by the scale
> of national inventory** — that how fast things actually "land" for people is
> paced by how much is in the system and how fast it flows, experienced in real,
> linear time. The premise names the steady holding-open of that pace
> **Sponsorship**: *the Sponsorship of the Principles of Delivery of the Method
> of Consciousness and Cooperation.*

Stated as an ordinary idea: *the real speed at which deliveries, decisions, and
outcomes reach people is governed by the economy's throughput and its stock of
goods-in-process — not by wish, and the work of keeping that pace steady (so
delivery stays conscious and cooperative) is worth naming.*

### 7.2 The terms, translated honestly

| Coined term | Plain meaning (as a metaphor) | Real thing it gestures toward |
|-------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Speed of the National Economy** | The rate at which things actually reach people, economy-wide. | Throughput / flow rate; velocity of money; inventory turnover. |
| **Throughput and time co-exist** | The amount flowing and the time it takes are two faces of one relation. | **Little's Law**: inventory = throughput × flow-time. |
| **Pace of scale of National Inventory** | The pace is set by how much is in the system. | Work-in-process / national inventory and the stocks–flows relation. |
| **Pace Sponsorship / real-time linear experience** | The steady upkeep that holds the pace, lived moment by moment. | Operating a system at steady state so flow time stays bounded. |
| **Principles of Delivery** | The rules by which things get where they're going, on time. | Operations, logistics, supply-chain flow. |
| **Method of Consciousness and Cooperation** | Delivery done *attentively and together* — not blindly, not alone. | Coordination / cooperative operation of a shared system. |

> Honest translation of the whole phrase: *"How fast things really arrive is
> paced by the economy's throughput and its inventory; keeping that pace steady,
> attentive, and cooperative is a real job worth naming."* That is a reasonable
> operational intuition. It is **not** a measured national constant, and this
> document does not pretend there is a "Speed of the National Economy" number.

### 7.3 The honest boundary (why there is no "Pace equation" here)

There is no single measured "speed of the national economy," no instrument that
returns one `Pace = ...` value, and no citable discoverer of such a law. Writing
one would invent a constant with no origin — forbidden by the one hard rule. So
the premise is recorded as a premise, and the **real** relation it points at is
cited instead (next).

### 7.4 Where the real economics actually lives (anchors you can cite)

The Pace premise gestures at genuine, citable operations economics:

- **Little's Law** — `L = lambda * W` (average inventory `L` = throughput
  `lambda` × average flow-time `W`). The exact, proven sense in which
  "throughput and time co-exist at the scale of inventory." *(John D. C. Little,
  "A Proof of the Queuing Formula L = λW," Operations Research, 1961.)*
- **Velocity of money** — from the equation of exchange `M V = P Q`; `V` is how
  fast money circulates, the monetary face of "the speed of the economy."
  *(Irving Fisher, 1911; already in `SMFinanceCanon`.)*
- **Inventory turnover** — `turnover = cost of goods sold / average inventory`;
  the standard operational measure of how fast a stock moves, i.e. its pace.

> Little's Law is a real theorem and could be added to `SMFinanceCanon` as a
> genuine, attributed entry (operations economics) — that is the faithful way to
> honor the throughput/time/inventory strand of this premise.

### 7.5 On "Consciousness and Cooperation"

That delivery should be *conscious* (attentive) and *cooperative* (done
together) is a **value about how systems ought to be run**, not a physical law.
It is recorded because the premise names it, and bounded for the same reason as
everything else here: a value statement is marked as a value statement.
