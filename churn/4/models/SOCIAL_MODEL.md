# /4 · Social model — the Area a working society spans

The **social model** here reads *area* as the extent a working society occupies,
and *density* as how tightly it fills it. Where `/2` (Structures) held the stock
of workers and `/3` (Facets) held the market's flow, `/4` (Areas) holds the
**ground** they stand on — the land the medium spans and packs.

## What the model is *for*

Area is the **ground** of the BASIS document ([`../BASIS.md`](../BASIS.md)), the
**Medium requirement for Workers, Work and Excellence — Workers**. A working
substrate does not float — it spans a real extent, and it must neither be
*depopulated* (too sparse to bear load) nor *over-packed* (too dense to
recover). The area-and-density figures give the requirement its literal footing:

- **Land area** is the extent the medium spans — the room the workers have.
- **Density** is how firmly the substrate is filled — enough presence to bear
  load and reconstruct a loss from a neighbor, without seizing.

Where `/2` asks *how many* workers and `/3` asks *how freely* they move, `/4`
asks *over how much ground* — the spatial face of an assailable-and-firm medium.

## The discipline the model keeps

Every social area is tagged **OBSERVED** and carries its **source** (density is
marked *derived* from population and land area). These are published statistics,
not definitions and not causal claims: `ASSOCIATION ≠ CAUSATION`. Relating area
to density, or density to the labor figures of `/2`–`/3`, is a **MODEL**, stated
to be argued with — never a fact asserted about any person or place.

## Provenance
