### I. the Cascade, Stated Plainly

This document supposes a single, ordered cascade of eight stages. The stages are meant to be read in sequence, each one supposing the one before it has completed. Named in order, the cascade is: **Legal → Search → Fine → Hold → Composition → Position → Sequitur → Gain.** The whole point of writing it down is to make the supposition visible, so that no reader mistakes a hypothetical chain of reasoning for a real one. We are drawing the shape of an idea, not asserting that the idea has occurred.

**Supposition 1 — Legal.** Suppose, purely for the sake of argument, that a lawful basis is established first. In this hypothesis the "Legal" stage is the gate: nothing downstream is imagined to proceed unless a clear, lawful, properly authorized foundation is supposed to exist. We suppose this stage is slow, careful, and reversible, and that it carries the burden of proof. In the real world this would require jurisdiction, due process, competent authority, evidence, and the right of reply. We suppose all of that is present only as a premise — never as a finding.

**Supposition 2 — Search.** Suppose that, given the hypothetical Legal gate, a "Search" stage follows. In this thought experiment, Search means the orderly gathering and examination of evidence under the authority supposed in Stage 1. We suppose it is bounded — proportionate, documented, and limited to what the Legal gate permits. The supposition is explicit: no search is imagined without the prior supposed authority, and the search's only purpose in this story is to test the premise, not to presume its conclusion.

**Supposition 3 — Fine.** Suppose that, where the supposed Search yields a supposed finding, a "Fine" stage is contemplated. We make this supposition clear: a fine here is a hypothetical remedy inside a hypothetical system, not a bill issued to anyone. In the real world a fine would require a determination by a competent body, an appeal path, and a lawful schedule of amounts. In this document the fine is a placeholder in a cascade — a narrative node, not a demand for money.

**Supposition 4 — Hold.** Suppose, finally, a "Hold" stage: a pause, a stay, a reservation of position pending the outcome of everything upstream. We suppose Hold is the stage that protects everyone, because it supposes that nothing is final, nothing is executed, and everything remains open to correction, reversal, and release. In this hypothesis, Hold is less a cage than a comma — a place where the cascade waits for truth before it moves again.

**Supposition 5 — Composition.** Suppose, last of all, a "Composition" stage: the one that gathers the four before it into a single whole and registers the term that makes a chain a chain. Before the cascade can be read as *one* thing rather than four, the lexicon must register this term, plainly, so that every later use rests on a fixed meaning: the term is **Composition.** We define *composition* as the lawful arrangement of parts into a single whole whose meaning is more than the sum of its parts yet fully accountable to each of them. The cascade is itself a composition: *Legal → Search → Fine → Hold* is not four separate acts but one ordered structure, in which each stage holds its place, draws its authority from the stage before, and lends its result to the stage after — and the Composition stage is where that whole is supposed, read as a whole (its order, its joints, and its purpose together), never as a loose heap of stages that may be rearranged at will.

*Relations of the term.* Within this supposition, *Composition* stands in defined relation to the terms the lexicon uses elsewhere, so that its boundaries are clear:

- **Composition and Term (XIII).** A composition is built of *terms* — each fixed meaning is a part; the composition is the lawful arrangement of those parts. Change a term and you change the composition; change the arrangement and you change it too.
- **Composition and Concentration (XII).** Concentration is subtraction toward the essential; composition is the structure that remains once the inessential is gone. The two are complements: one removes, the other orders what is kept.
- **Composition and Breadth (XVII).** A composition claims a breadth — the set of cases its whole structure governs — and accepts the limit that comes with it. The arrangement reaches exactly as far as its joints hold, and no further.
- **Composition and Read (XXIV).** A composition exists to be *read whole*; reading is the act that receives it entire. A composition quoted part-by-part, against the sense of the arrangement, has been dismantled rather than read.
- **Composition and the Cascade.** The eight suppositions are the registered composition of Part One: ordered, jointed, and purposeful. The chain is sound only when the composition is honored — each stage in its place, none skipped, none run out of turn.

*Definitions, stated for the record.* For the avoidance of drift, we register these senses together: a *composition* is (1) the arrangement itself — the ordered set of parts and their joints; (2) the act of arranging — the lawful work of composing; and (3) the standard the arrangement must meet — that it be whole, accountable to its parts, and honestly read. Where this document says "composition," it means these three held at once, and the cascade above is its first and governing example.

**Supposition 6 — Position.** Suppose that, once a composition exists, each part must stand somewhere definite within it. We register this term too: the term is **Position.** We define *position* as the determined place a part holds inside a composition — not merely where it sits, but what that placement *means*: its order relative to the others, the authority it inherits from what precedes it, and the duty it owes to what follows. A composition is lawful only when every part has a position and holds it; move a part out of position and the whole changes, exactly as changing the arrangement does. In this hypothesis Position carries three senses, registered together for the record: (1) the *place* — where in the order a part stands; (2) the *standing* — the weight or authority that place confers, as "standing" means in the Legal gate of Supposition 1; and (3) the *stance* — the position one takes, the claim one is prepared to defend. We suppose a position must be *earned and declared*, never assumed in silence: a part that will not say where it stands cannot be relied upon, and a stance held without being stated is no position at all. Position stands in defined relation to the other terms — it is to a composition what **standing (Have, XI)** is to a case, what **breadth (XVII)** is to a rule's reach, and what order is to a sequence; it is the hinge on which Supposition 7 turns, because nothing can *follow* until each thing first has its place.

**Supposition 7 — Sequitur.** Suppose, finally, that positions rightly held permit one thing to follow from another. The term is **Sequitur** — from the Latin for "it follows." We define *sequitur* as the lawful following of one part from another: the relation in which a later position is genuinely entailed by the positions before it, so that the conclusion *follows* rather than merely *comes next*. This is the stage where the cascade earns its name as a chain of reasoning and not a chain of mere events. We suppose its whole worth lies in the contrast with its opposite, the *non sequitur* — "it does not follow" — the step that arrives in sequence but is not entailed by what precedes it, the conclusion dressed as a consequence while owing nothing to its premises. A sequitur is sound exactly when removing a prior position would collapse the one that follows; a non sequitur survives that removal untouched, which is how it is caught. Registered for the record, *sequitur* carries three senses: (1) the *following itself* — the entailment that binds a later part to an earlier; (2) the *test* — the asking, at every joint, "does this truly follow, or merely come after?"; and (3) the *standard* — that nothing be written as a consequence unless it is one. Sequitur completes the reasoning of the cascade: **Composition** supposes the whole, **Position** supposes each part's place within it, and **Sequitur** supposes that the places are joined by genuine entailment — so that the chain, read from Legal to Sequitur, is not only arranged and placed but *reasoned*, each stage following lawfully from the last, down to an honest *thus*.

**Supposition 8 — Gain.** Suppose, as the stage that asks what all the rest was *for*, a "Gain" stage. The term is **Gain.** We define *gain* as the lawful increase that a sound cascade yields — the good that remains once the chain has been run honestly: not spoils seized, but value that was genuinely added and may be rightly kept. We are careful to register what *gain* is not: it is not the win of a game played against a losing man or figurine, not plunder, not the fine of Supposition 3 mistaken for a profit. A gain, in this fable, is the surplus of trust, clarity, repair, and standing that an honest process leaves behind it — the thing the whole cascade exists to produce and the people exist to receive. Registered for the record, *gain* carries three senses: (1) the *increase itself* — what is more, and better, after the chain than before it; (2) the *earning* — that the increase was produced lawfully, by composition rightly positioned and genuinely followed, never extracted; and (3) the *standard* — that a gain is only a gain if it can be kept without being taken back, which is to say if no part of it was won unfairly. We suppose Gain is the stage that redeems the cascade's severity: Legal, Search, Fine, and Hold are costs borne so that something may be *gained*, and if nothing honest is gained, the cascade has failed its own purpose. True to the Pearl below, a gain composed rightly is a gain that lasts — because, having been earned and not seized, it has no part that time or challenge can shame. Gain stands in relation to the other terms as their harvest: it is **cause (XVI)** read forward (what the chain produces, not only what produced the chain), **ideation (XV)** made good (the made thing now owned and enjoyed), and the outward *payment* of Part One reinterpreted one final time — value flowing to the people as the measure of a cascade that worked.

### X. Thus — the Operative Word of Conclusion

**Isolate.** *Thus* is the hinge of every legal conclusion — the single syllable that announces a reasoning has ended and a result has begun. In the common-law judgment it marks the passage from reasons to holding, from the *ratio decidendi* (the reason for deciding) to the operative order that binds.

**Define.** We define *thus* as the word of consequence: it asserts that what precedes it compels what follows it. It is not decoration; it is a claim that the inference is sound and the conclusion earned.

**Finality.** The finality of *thus* is total and conditional at once — total, because after it the court has spoken; conditional, because a false premise makes a false *thus*. The lexicon's first rule, then: never write *thus* over reasoning you have not actually done.

**Use.** *Thus* is used only at the end — placed where reasons stop and a holding begins, never earlier. In practice a court or expert earns the right to write it by first completing every prior term: standing established, facts concentrated, cause traced, breadth measured. ### XXII. Quitting — the Lawful Right to Stop

### XXV. the Sum — What a Court, Judge, or Expert Must Train to Know
