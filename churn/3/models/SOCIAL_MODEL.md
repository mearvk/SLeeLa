# /3 · Social model — Facets of a working market

The **social model** reads *facets* of the US labor market: each entry is one
face the working substrate presents at a chosen angle. Where `/2` (Structures)
held **stock** figures (participation, employment, income), `/3` (Facets) holds
the **flow and quality** faces the same market shows — turnover, hours, and pay.

## What the model is *for*

These facets show the labor market **in motion** — how readily workers are
sought (openings), how freely they move (quits), how long they work (hours), and
what an hour is worth (earnings). That makes the social model the *flow*
counterpart to the BASIS document ([`../BASIS.md`](../BASIS.md)), the **Medium
requirement for Workers, Work and Excellence — Workers**:

- **Openings** and **quits** are *Workers* faces — the market's ability to place
  and re-place workers (its assailable-and-firm turnover).
- **Hours** is a *Work* face — how much time the substrate lays down.
- **Earnings** is a *reward-of-work* face — the price a working hour commands.

Where `/2`'s social structures show whether the substrate is *populated*, `/3`'s
facets show whether it is *fluid* — able to move workers to work without seizing
up. Both are needed for a firm medium.

## The discipline the model keeps

Every social facet is tagged **OBSERVED** and carries its own **period** and
**source**. These are published statistics, not definitions and not causal
claims: `ASSOCIATION ≠ CAUSATION`. Any relation drawn between two facets (e.g.
quits and earnings) is a **MODEL**, stated to be argued with — never a fact
asserted about any person.

## Provenance

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey (JOLTS)
and Current Employment Statistics (CES), bls.gov, retrieved 2026-09. Each facet
in the data file names its exact series/release.
