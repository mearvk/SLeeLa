# 07 — Greece, BC to 1600: events, concessions, and weighings

*A Greek-specific timeline from the archaic period to 1600 CE. Each event is
**noted** (what happened), its **concession** is recorded (what, if anything,
Greece yielded to an outside calendar authority), and it is given a **weighing**
— a 1–10 weight scoring its contribution to the lineage that ends in the Unified
Calendar (document 99), with a one-line rationale.*

This document extends document 00 (the Greek lunisolar principle) into a dated,
weighed record, and it resolves a specific boundary fact: **by 1600, Greece had
*not* adopted the Gregorian reform of 1582** — it remained on the Julian /
Orthodox reckoning. That non-adoption is itself the final, heavily-weighed event
of the span.

## How to read the weighing

- **Weight (1–10):** contribution to the Unified Calendar lineage — i.e. how much
  the event shaped the Greek → Julian → Gregorian → global-civil through-line.
  10 = a load-bearing contribution still present in the modern calendar; 1 = a
  local event with negligible downstream effect.
- **Concession:** what Greek reckoning *yielded to*. `native` means Greek-origin,
  no concession; otherwise the external standard adopted/imposed is named.

## Concessions, summarized

Greece's calendar history across this span is a sequence of **three concessions**
wrapped around **one refusal**:

1. **146 BC — political concession:** calendar authority passes to Rome.
2. **46/45 BC — civil concession:** local lunisolar yields to the **Julian**
   calendar (built, notably, on Greek science).
3. **Byzantine era — framing concession:** Anno Mundi count and 1 September
   year-start layered over Julian days.
4. **1582 — the refusal:** Greece declines the Gregorian reform and **retains
   Julian**, a stance held through 1600 and far beyond (full civil Gregorian
   adoption comes only in 1923, outside this span).

## Weighing, in aggregate

The heaviest Greek contributions to the Unified Calendar lineage are the
**Metonic cycle (10)** and the **Nicaea/Alexandrian computus (9)** — Greek
mathematics that is *still executing* inside the modern calendar's Easter rule —
and the two posture-defining events, the **45 BC Julian concession (9)** and the
**1582 refusal (10)**, which together place Greece as the civilization that
*originated* the solar year's arithmetic yet was the *last major holder* of the
Julian baseline. That tension — Greek science inside the calendar, Greek practice
outside the reform — is the weighed core this document contributes to the set.
