# ENCLOSURE.md — Country Standards Enclosure

**Status:** Guiding comparative framework for the 391 world entries in `BYPASS.md`  
**Parent document:** `BYPASS.md`  
**Purpose:** Provide a consistent enclosure for Internet, technology, economic-system, and civic-kindness observations for every country, territory, historical entry, or polity represented by the parent document.

## 1. The Country Enclosure

Each country entry in `BYPASS.md` may be understood as a parent enclosure containing a common set of comparative observations. The enclosure is designed to keep technical standards, economic institutions, and civic values together without treating a population as politically or morally uniform.

The enclosure should preserve the existing **Secular Civic Wisdom** paragraph and add the following four standards beneath it:

1. **Internet & Technology Standard**
2. **Technology Grade — 1–4**
3. **Socialism Standard**
4. **Kind / Civic Standard**

These elements are descriptive project fields. They are not intended to establish legal status, assign personal political beliefs, or rank the human worth of one population against another.

## 2. Internet & Technology Standard

**Internet & Technology Standard** describes the practical digital environment of the entry. It may include Internet availability, mobile and fixed broadband, network quality, affordability, digital skills, devices, cybersecurity, digital government, technical education, data infrastructure, and international interoperability.

The principal methodological reference is the International Telecommunication Union's **ICT Development Index (IDI)** and its Universal and Meaningful Connectivity framework. ITU describes meaningful connectivity in terms of people being able to access the Internet under optimal conditions, at an affordable cost, anytime and anywhere. The 2026 IDI covers 159 economies, so the 391-entry project necessarily extends beyond the economies for which an official IDI score is available. citeturn0search3turn0search5

Where an official country-level metric is unavailable, the enclosure should say **Not Available / Project Assessment** rather than inventing a numerical statistic. Territories, dependencies, uninhabited locations, and historical entries should be treated according to the infrastructure that actually exists or existed rather than being forced into a modern sovereign-state model.

## 3. Technology Grade — 1–4

The project technology grade is a simple four-level reference scale:

| Grade | Project Meaning |
|---|---|
| **4** | United States reference level: highly developed, broadly available, sophisticated digital infrastructure and technology ecosystem |
| **3** | Advanced or strongly developed national/territorial technology environment, with meaningful remaining gaps or narrower scale |
| **2** | Developing or mixed technology environment, with substantial differences in access, quality, affordability, skills, or infrastructure |
| **1** | Limited technology environment, with major infrastructure, affordability, access, skills, or reliability constraints |

**Important:** Grade 4 is the project's United States reference point; it does **not** mean that the United States is officially ranked first by every international technology measure. Likewise, a lower grade is not a judgment about the intelligence, dignity, or capability of a country's people.

The grade should be based on observable infrastructure and institutional capacity rather than stereotypes. ITU's current work emphasizes universal and meaningful connectivity and identifies quality, availability, affordability, devices, skills, and security as important dimensions. citeturn0search7turn0search11

## 4. Socialism Standard

**Socialism Standard** describes the economic and institutional presence of socialist, social-democratic, state-owned, centrally planned, or mixed-market arrangements. It should distinguish between:

- explicit socialist constitutional or governmental structures;
- extensive state ownership or central planning;
- social-democratic welfare institutions operating within a market economy;
- ordinary public services that exist in many different economic systems; and
- predominantly private or market-oriented systems.

This field is **not** a label for the beliefs of individual citizens. A country may provide extensive public healthcare, education, pensions, infrastructure, or social insurance while retaining a predominantly private market economy. The enclosure should describe the institutions rather than collapse those distinctions into a single political label.

Suggested project vocabulary:

- **S-4 — Explicit / Extensive Socialist State Structure**
- **S-3 — Strong State or Social-Democratic Economic Role**
- **S-2 — Mixed Public/Private Economic Structure**
- **S-1 — Predominantly Market-Oriented Structure**
- **S-N/A — Not Applicable or Insufficient Evidence**

The S-grade is independent of the technology grade. A country may therefore be technologically advanced and strongly social-democratic, technologically advanced and market-oriented, or technologically developing under either a predominantly public or private economic structure.

## 5. Kind / Civic Standard

**Kind / Civic Standard** is a non-personal civic measure. It describes the extent to which the country's institutions, laws, public programs, and civic ideals expressly support concepts such as human dignity, peaceful coexistence, education, accessibility, public welfare, protection of vulnerable persons, freedom of conscience, truthful public information, and responsible stewardship.

It must **not** assert that citizens of a country are inherently kind or unkind. Human beings within every entry are diverse. The field concerns public ideals and institutional practices, not the moral character of an entire population.

Suggested project vocabulary:

- **K-4 — Strong Institutional/Civic Kindness Orientation**
- **K-3 — Substantial Civic and Welfare Orientation**
- **K-2 — Mixed or Uneven Institutional Orientation**
- **K-1 — Limited or Weak Institutional Expression**
- **K-N/A — Not Applicable or Insufficient Evidence**

The grade should be accompanied by a short explanation when the evidence is contested, incomplete, historically changing, or difficult to compare across jurisdictions.

## 6. Standard Country Entry Form

Each parent country enclosure should follow this pattern:

### **NNN. COUNTRY / ENTRY NAME**

**Secular Civic Wisdom.** Preserve the existing wisdom paragraph from `BYPASS.md`, including its emphasis on dignity, learning, truthful inquiry, responsible institutions, stewardship, peaceful disagreement, and respect for internal diversity.

**Internet & Technology Standard.** Describe the Internet, telecommunications, digital infrastructure, access, affordability, skills, security, and interoperability environment.

**Technology Grade — N/4.** Assign the project's 1–4 grade, with **4 = United States reference level**, and briefly explain the principal infrastructure and capacity considerations.

**Socialism Standard — S-N.** Describe the country's economic structure, including the relationship between state ownership, public services, planning, private markets, and social-democratic institutions where relevant.

**Kind / Civic Standard — K-N.** Describe institutional and civic commitments concerning dignity, welfare, peace, accessibility, education, conscience, public responsibility, and treatment of vulnerable persons without making a blanket judgment about the population.

## 7. Evidence and Uncertainty

The enclosure should prefer authoritative international measurements, national statistical agencies, telecommunications regulators, constitutional documents, and reputable institutional research. The ITU's current statistics demonstrate why a single connectivity number is insufficient: global connectivity varies by quality, affordability, usage, devices, skills, security, income, geography, and other factors. In 2025, for example, ITU reported that approximately three-quarters of the world's population was online while substantial affordability and quality gaps remained. citeturn0search2turn0search10

Where evidence is incomplete, the correct response is to state the uncertainty. The enclosure should never manufacture precision merely to fill a field.

## 8. Historical and Non-Sovereign Entries

The 391-entry parent document deliberately includes more than currently recognized sovereign states. Therefore, an enclosure may represent a territory, dependency, special jurisdiction, historical country, polity, or geographic entry. Such an entry should not be described as a sovereign state merely because it has a country-style heading.

For locations without a permanent population or independent Internet infrastructure, the Internet & Technology Standard should explain the exceptional status rather than assigning a misleading ordinary-country score.

## 9. Comparative Principle

The four dimensions are deliberately independent:

**Technology is not Socialism.**  
**Socialism is not Kindness.**  
**Kindness is not Technology.**  
**A country's infrastructure is not a measure of the worth of its people.**

The purpose of the enclosure is to make comparisons legible while preserving human dignity and institutional nuance.

## 10. Relationship to `BYPASS.md`

`BYPASS.md` remains the parent country-by-country civic wisdom document. `ENCLOSURE.md` supplies the reusable structure for expanding each parent entry with technical, economic, and civic-standard information.

The intended relationship is:

**BYPASS.md → Country Entry → ENCLOSURE.md Fields → Evidence → Comparative Grade**

This structure permits the 391 entries to grow consistently without requiring every country to be treated as identical, sovereign, economically organized in the same way, or technologically measured by a statistic that does not exist for that entry.

## 11. Guiding Principle

The enclosure exists to make knowledge clearer, not to make people smaller. Every grade is provisional, every comparison should be evidence-based, and every country entry should retain the recognition that human beings and their institutions are more complex than a four-level scale.

**Technical clarity, institutional distinction, civic dignity, and honest uncertainty are the governing principles of the Country Enclosure.**
